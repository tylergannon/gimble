# The chapter doc

A chapter is a durable vector for a run of sprints, not a task list.
Write one chapter doc per chapter, short enough that a sprint planner
can read every chapter's index before planning.

Structure, in order:

1. Title, status, and one paragraph: what exists when the chapter is
   done and which promises it serves.
2. Pyramid index (see the pyramid-index page): L0, L1, L2.
3. Vector: the direction sprints in this chapter push.
4. Design direction: themes to preserve.
5. Architecture principles: boundaries, sources of truth, what may not
   move.
6. Sprint horizon: the kinds of sprint that belong here. Bullets, not a
   backlog; the backlog lives in the sprint ledger.
7. Review posture: how a planner or verifier tells the chapter is
   advancing, and what the verifier will operate at chapter exit.
8. Non-goals: what this chapter deliberately leaves out.
9. Sprint planning notes: instructions the next planner carries.

Keep it under about two thousand words. Principles and boundaries over
inventories of tasks. Do not put anything in a chapter doc that is
fine-grained enough to belong in a sprint doc.

The chapter ledger item points at this doc and at the chapter's sprint
ledger. The chapter is edited only with a reason, recorded where the
edit is made.
